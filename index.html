SHEET A-200 · METHOD

How I think.

Security is drawn before it's deployed. Three working drawings from the table — hover anything to interrogate it.

FIG. 01 — PLAN VIEW

Trust is drawn, not assumed.

A zero-trust estate, drafted as a floor plan. Identity is the gatehouse.

Untrusted exterior

Everything outside is hostile weather. Assume scanning, phishing, and probing as the ambient condition.

Edge / DMZ

Filtering, rate-limiting, and exposure kept deliberately thin. The wall matters — but nobody trusts a wall alone anymore.

Identity gatehouse

The real front door. Strong auth, just-in-time access, continuous verification. Every entry is checked, every time.

Micro-segmented rooms

Workloads live in separate rooms with the internal doors shut. An intruder in one room does not get the house.

Telemetry spine

A corridor of observability running through everything. If a room can't report, it doesn't get built.

Data vault

Thickest walls in the drawing. Encryption, classification, and loss prevention — the answer to "what are we actually protecting?"

FIG. 02 — SECTION

Detection is a refinery.

Billions in, one truth out. Tuning is the craft; logging is just the quarry.

01Collect wide

Visibility precedes defense. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen — and the attacker knows it.

02Normalize ruthlessly

Parse, enrich, deduplicate. Raw telemetry is ore, not metal — refinement starts at ingestion.

03Correlate for behavior

Single events lie; sequences confess. Map detections to adversary behavior, not just signatures.

04Tune or drown

Every false positive is a tax on the analyst. Alert fatigue is a design failure, not a staffing one.

05Investigate with hypotheses

Hunting is science: propose, test, conclude. Intuition guided by intelligence, recorded either way.

06Confirm, then encode

A true positive is a gift. The lesson goes back into the refinery so next time takes minutes, not days.

FIG. 03 — DETAIL

Break the chain early.

Every intrusion is a sequence. Hover a link to see what cuts it.

01 · Reconnaissance — deny them the map.

Attack-surface management, minimal public footprint, honeytokens that make scouting expensive.

02 · Weaponization — watch the forges.

Largely attacker-side, so defense is intelligence: track tooling, TTPs, and the infrastructure being staged.

03 · Delivery — filter the post.

Mail and web gateways, sandbox detonation, user reporting culture. Most chains die here — cheaply.

04 · Exploitation — close the window.

Patch cadence, hardening baselines, exploit mitigations. Every closed vulnerability is a link that can't form.

05 · Installation — refuse the tenant.

EDR, application control, persistence-hunting. The implant that can't survive a reboot was never really there.

06 · Command & control — cut the phone line.

Egress filtering, DNS analytics, beacon detection. Malware without its operator is just a file.

07 · Actions on objectives — guard the vault.

Segmentation, least privilege, DLP. Even a completed chain should find the prize out of reach.
